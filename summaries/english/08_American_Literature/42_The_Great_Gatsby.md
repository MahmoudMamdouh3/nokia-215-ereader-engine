# Comprehensive Study Guide: The Great Gatsby
**Author:** F. Scott Fitzgerald  
**Year:** 1925  
**Genre:** Modernist Classic / The Great American Novel  

---

## 1. Historical & Cultural Context
Set in the summer of 1922 during the Roaring Twenties—an era defined by Prohibition, unprecedented economic prosperity, bootlegging, jazz music, and decaying Victorian social morals. Fitzgerald crafted an unforgettable critique of the American Dream, depicting how boundless ambition devolved into reckless consumerism, moral carelessness, and spiritual emptiness.

---

## 2. In-Depth Chapter-by-Chapter Plot Breakdown

### Chapters 1–3: The Landscape of Wealth and Illusion
- **Nick Carraway's Arrival**: Nick Carraway, an educated Midwesterner, moves to West Egg on Long Island to sell bonds. He rents a modest cottage neighboring the colossal, faux-Norman mansion of Jay Gatsby.
- **The Buchanans at East Egg**: Nick visits his cousin Daisy Buchanan and her brutish, immensely wealthy husband Tom at East Egg (representing old, inherited aristocratic money). There, he meets the cynical golfer Jordan Baker and learns of Tom's mistress in New York.
- **The Valley of Ashes**: Traveling into the city with Tom, Nick passes through the Valley of Ashes—a desolate industrial wasteland watched over by the faded billboard billboard eyes of Doctor T. J. Eckleburg. They visit Tom's mistress, Myrtle Wilson, at her husband George's garage. At an impromptu party in an apartment, Tom violently breaks Myrtle's nose when she repeats Daisy's name.
- **Gatsby's Extravagant Parties**: Nick attends one of Gatsby's lavish weekend parties, where hundreds of uninvited guests consume bootleg liquor. Rumors abound about Gatsby's origins (a German spy, an Oxford man, an assassin). Nick finally meets Gatsby, a remarkably gracious host with an unforgettable smile, who watches the green light at the end of Daisy's dock across the dark bay.

### Chapters 4–6: The Quest for Daisy
- **Gatsby's Fabrications**: Driving to lunch with Nick, Gatsby presents an exaggerated autobiography of war medals and Oxford education, confirmed when gambler Meyer Wolfsheim brags about Gatsby and reveals he fixed the 1919 World Series.
- **The Tea at Nick's**: Jordan reveals to Nick that Gatsby bought his mansion specifically to be across the bay from Daisy, and organized all his lavish parties hoping she would wander in. Nick arranges an afternoon tea for Daisy and Gatsby. After initial excruciating awkwardness, their passion reignites; Gatsby displays his mountain of imported silk shirts, bringing Daisy to tears over what she missed.
- **James Gatz Revealed**: Fitzgerald reveals Gatsby's true history: born James Gatz to impoverished North Dakota farmers, he reinvented himself under the mentorship of millionaire Dan Cody, dedicating his entire life to becoming wealthy enough to possess Daisy.

### Chapters 7–9: Climax, Tragedy, and Reckoning
- **The Confrontation at the Plaza Hotel**: On the hottest day of summer, Nick, Jordan, Gatsby, Tom, and Daisy gather at the Plaza Hotel in New York. Gatsby demands Daisy tell Tom she never loved him. Daisy hesitates, breaking down: *"I did love him once—but I loved you too."* Realizing his victory, Tom smugly exposes Gatsby's criminal bootlegging operations, shattering Daisy's illusion.
- **The Fatal Accident**: Returning to Long Island in Gatsby's yellow car, Daisy drives in an agitated state. Myrtle Wilson, mistaking the yellow car for Tom's, rushes into the street and is struck and killed instantly. Gatsby hides outside Daisy's house that night to protect her, revealing to Nick that Daisy was at the wheel, but he will take the blame.
- **The Murder of Gatsby**: Consumed by grief and manipulated by Tom Buchanan into believing Gatsby was both Myrtle's lover and killer, George Wilson walks to West Egg, shoots Gatsby dead in his swimming pool, and commits suicide.
- **The Hollow Funeral**: Nick attempts to gather Gatsby's friends for his funeral. None of the hundreds of socialites, business partners, or partygoers show up; Daisy and Tom vanish without leaving an address. Only Nick, Gatsby's heartbroken elderly father Henry Gatz, and the owl-eyed man attend.
- **Conclusion**: Disgusted by the moral decay of the East, Nick prepares to return to the Midwest. He walks to Gatsby's deserted dock, contemplating how humanity strives against fate: *"So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past."*

## 3. Major Themes & Symbolism
- **The Corruption of the American Dream**: The dream of self-reinvention and meritocracy is exposed as an empty facade corrupted by criminal wealth and impenetrable old-money class prejudice.
- **The Green Light**: Symbolizes Gatsby's yearning, the unattainable future, and the universal human desire to reach toward an idealized dream.
- **The Eyes of Dr. T.J. Eckleburg**: The giant faded spectacle billboard looming over the Valley of Ashes symbolizes the dead, absent gaze of God over a spiritually hollow, commercialized wasteland.
- **Carelessness of the Wealthy**: As Nick famously concludes, Tom and Daisy were careless people who smashed up things and creatures and then retreated back into their money.
